Data Detectives Week — Teacher / Parent Guide

Theme: Science & Graphing  |  Audience: Grades 1–2  |  Narrator: Dot the Ladybug

Causal Arc: Observe & Count → Record in a Table → Pictograph → Bar Graph → Full Experiment & Interpret

Standards: CCSS.Math 1.MD.C.4 & 2.MD.D.10 (represent and interpret data); NGSS K–2 science practices (planning investigations; analyzing & interpreting data).

Many activities are open-ended experiments (Ramp Race, Paper Airplanes) and the "make a graph" pages. For those, there is no single answer key — instead, check that the child's bars/pictures match the numbers they recorded in their data table, that bars start at the bottom line (zero) and are the same width, and that the graph has a title and labels.

Monday — Data Detectives (Observe, Count, Record)

Answer Key — Reading Questions

Q1 (Data): Data is facts you collect by watching and counting. Example: the number of red cars that drive by, how many birds you see, etc.

Q2 (Tally marks): They help you count quickly and keep your place; grouping by fives ( |||| ) makes big counts easy.

Q3 (Recording): So you do not forget the data. Scientists must be able to look back at what they found.

Counting Experiment (T-Chart)

Open-ended. Child picks one thing to count and records tally marks + a total per row. Check that the total matches the tally marks (count the fives). Great chance to reinforce counting by fives.

LET'S DISCUSS Guidance

"If Dot counted 20 bugs but did not write it down…" — She would likely forget the exact number. Reinforce: recording data is what separates guessing from real science.

Misconception to Watch

Some children draw the fifth tally as a separate line instead of the cross-line ( |||| ). Model the group-of-five so totals are easy to read.

Extension

Count two things at once (e.g., cars vs. trucks for 5 minutes) to set up tomorrow's idea of comparing groups on a graph.

Tuesday — Pictographs

Answer Key — Reading Questions

Q1: Pictures (a pictograph uses pictures to show data).

Q2: The key tells what one picture is worth — without it you can't know the real numbers.

Q3: The group with the most pictures / the longest row is the biggest.

Read Dot's Ladybug Graph (each 🐜 = 1)

Most: Wednesday (6).   Fewest: Thursday (2).

How many more Wed than Thu: 6 − 2 = 4.

Monday + Tuesday: 5 + 3 = 8.

Make Your Own Pictograph

Roses = 4 🌸, Tulips = 6 🌸, Daisies = 3 🌸. Longest row = Tulips (the most flowers). Check that the child draws exactly the right number in each row.

LET'S DISCUSS Guidance

Key = 2 bugs, row has 4 pictures → 4 × 2 = 8 bugs. If the key were 5 each: 4 × 5 = 20. Plants the seed for Thursday's scaled pictograph.

Misconception to Watch

Children may ignore the key and just count pictures. Keep pointing back to the key: "What is one picture worth?"

Extension

Turn Monday's counting data into a pictograph on scrap paper — their own data becomes a graph.

Wednesday — Bar Graphs (Ramp Race)

Answer Key — Reading Questions

Q1: A taller bar means a bigger number (more).

Q2: Bottom axis = the groups you are counting; side axis = the numbers (how many).

Q3: The title tells you what the graph is about, so the bars have meaning.

Ramp Race Experiment + Bar Graph

Open-ended data. Typical result: the higher the ramp, the farther the car rolls, so bars increase Low → Medium → High. Check that each bar's height matches the steps recorded in the data table, bars are equal width, and each starts at the zero line.

Graph Q's: Farthest = usually the High ramp. As the ramp got higher, the car rolled farther (more starting height → more speed → more distance).

LET'S DISCUSS Guidance

Graph vs. a list of numbers: The bar graph is faster — the tallest bar is visible instantly, while a list must be read and compared number by number.

Misconceptions to Watch

Bars floating above the line, uneven widths, or gaps of different sizes. Also stress the fair test: same car, same push (just release it), only the ramp height changes.

Extension

Re-run with the SAME ramp height but a different surface (carpet vs. wood). Add those bars — which surface let the car roll farther?

Thursday — Reading & Comparing Graphs

Answer Key — Reading Questions

Q1 (Four questions): Which is the most? Which is the least? How many more? How many altogether?

Q2 (How many more): Find both numbers and subtract the smaller from the bigger.

Seed Bar Graph (Shade 2, Some sun 5, Full sun 8)

Most: Full sun (8).   Fewest: Shade (2).

More Full sun than Shade: 8 − 2 = 6.

Altogether: 2 + 5 + 8 = 15.

What plants need: more sunlight helps more seeds sprout / grow.

Garden Bug Pictograph (each 🐛 = 2)

Ants: 4 × 2 = 8.   Bees: 2 × 2 = 4.   Worms: 3 × 2 = 6.

More ants than bees: 8 − 4 = 4.

Altogether: 8 + 4 + 6 = 18 bugs.

LET'S DISCUSS Guidance

"A graph is a story." A bedtime graph's tallest bar = the night with the latest bedtime. Encourage the child to narrate what the graph "says" happened.

Misconception to Watch

On the bug pictograph, children often answer 4, 2, 3 (the picture counts) instead of 8, 4, 6. Anchor every answer to the key: "each picture = 2, so…"

Extension

Ask "how many fewer bees than ants?" and "how many more ants than worms?" to practice comparison in both directions.

Friday — Be a Data Scientist (Capstone)

Answer Key — Reading Questions

Q1 (Five steps): Ask → Do → Record → Graph → Read.

Q2 (Question): "Which paper airplane flies the farthest?"

Paper Airplane Contest (data table + bar graph)

Open-ended data. Check the full cycle: the data table has a distance for each plane; the bar graph's bars match those numbers (equal width, starting at zero, titled); and the child reads their OWN graph to answer.

Graph Q's: Farthest / shortest = whichever plane the child's bars show. "How many more" = subtract the shortest from the farthest. The ANSWER to Dot's question = the name of the tallest bar.

LET'S DISCUSS Guidance

Fair test: same person throwing, same starting line, same amount of push, same paper size. Only the plane design changes.

Misconception to Watch

Throwing planes different numbers of times or from different spots, then comparing — that is not a fair test. One throw each from the same line keeps it fair (or best-of-three, but the same for every plane).

Extension

Throw each plane THREE times and graph the best distance — introduces the idea of repeating a trial for more trustworthy data.

Week Summary — The Data Cycle

  1. Monday: Observe and count; record data with tally marks in a table.
  2. Tuesday: Show data as a pictograph; read the key; make one.
  3. Wednesday: Show data as a bar graph; axes, title; graph a real experiment.
  4. Thursday: Read graphs — most, least, how many more (subtract), altogether (add); scaled key.
  5. Friday: Run a full experiment — ask, do, record, graph, read — and interpret your own results.

By Friday, the child should be able to collect a small set of data, build a titled bar graph or pictograph whose sizes match the data, and answer "which is most/least," "how many more," and "how many altogether."
